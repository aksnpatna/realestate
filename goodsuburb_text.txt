Feature Story · Middle Melbourne · VIC
Point Cook: suburb intelligence for first home buyers
Point Cook is a mixed picture for first home buyers — scoring 58/100 on GoodSuburb's rating, with strong schools (grade A+) and good transit access. Point Cook is one of the larger suburbs in Melbourne's middle ring, sitting 20.8km from the CBD with around 66,781 residents. Median household income runs $2,392/week — well above the Victorian median ($1,562/wk), with a younger-than-average age profile at a median age of 33. The 11 schools within the suburb boundary average top-tier schools (ICSEA 1141, 92th percentile nationally). Transit score is 51/100 — reasonable coverage for a suburban area, though peak-hour frequency varies by line. Owner-occupancy sits at 65.5%. First home buyers building new may qualify for the $10,000 FHOG grant in this suburb.

Why buyers consider Point Cook
Buyers are drawn to Point Cook's top-performing schools, with an ICSEA score of 1088, making it an attractive option for families. The suburb's relatively affordable median house price of $800,000, combined with the $10,000 First Home Owner Grant, makes it an appealing choice for first-home buyers. Point Cook's diverse community, with a strong Indian influence, adds to its appeal, offering a unique cultural experience.
Trade-offs to consider
While Point Cook offers a desirable lifestyle, buyers should be aware of its relatively poor public transport options, with a transit score of 27/100. The suburb's growth corridor status, while beneficial for long-term investment, may also lead to increased prices and competition for buyers. Additionally, the 20.8km distance from Melbourne's CBD may be a drawback for some, requiring a longer commute.

About Point Cook
Point Cook is one of the more populous suburbs in VIC, home to 66,781 people in Melbourne’s middle ring, sitting 20.8km from the CBD. Median household income sits at $2,392 per week — notably higher than the VIC median, reflecting an affluent professional community. The median age of 33 years suggests a mix of young families and established professionals setting down roots.
Schools in Point Cook
School quality in Point Cook is graded A+, based on an average ICSEA score of 1,141 — placing the suburb around the 98th percentile nationally. ICSEA (Index of Community Socio-Educational Advantage) measures the socio-educational background of school communities. The national average is 1,000; the VIC metropolitan median is approximately 1027. Alamanda K-9 College is the highest-rated school within the suburb boundary, a combined school with an ICSEA score of 1,141. There are 11 schools within or immediately adjacent to Point Cook, providing choice across year levels and schooling types. Source: ACARA School Profiles 2025.
Getting around Point Cook
Point Cook has reasonable public transport access, particularly for peak-hour commutes. The suburb scores 51/100 on the GoodSuburb transit index, which is calculated from the density of train, tram and bus stops within the suburb boundary using real GTFS data from PTV (June 2026). At 20.8km from Melbourne CBD, public transport is a viable option for city-bound commuters, though peak hour services vary. The suburb is served by FTTB NBN, providing residential internet connectivity.
Property prices in Point Cook
The median house price in Point Cook is $800k — near the VIC median. Prices have risen 3.9% over the past year. These figures are sourced from the Valuer General Victoria and represent median residential sale prices across all recorded transactions. First home buyers purchasing a new home in Point Cook may be eligible for the First Home Owner Grant of $10,000. Check eligibility and apply at sro.vic.gov.au. Note that the FHOG applies to new and substantially renovated homes only — not established properties.
Who lives in Point Cook
The community in Point Cook is predominantly Australian-born (43.9%), with a notable overseas-born population (56%) reflecting Melbourne’s multicultural character. India-born residents make up 14.9% of the population. The most common industry of employment for Point Cook residents is Professional & Scientific (14.3% of employed residents), followed by Health & Social Services. Owner-occupancy sits at 65.5% — a high rate suggesting a stable, long-term resident community. All community data is sourced from ABS Census 2021.

What these numbers mean
ICSEA
Index of Community Socio-Educational Advantage. Measures the social and educational background of a school's families. National average is 1,000. Higher = more advantaged community.
%ile
Percentile ranking among all Australian schools. 94th %ile means the school's community is more advantaged than 94% of all schools nationally.
Top SEA
Top Socio-Educational Advantage quarter. The percentage of students from the most advantaged 25% of Australian families. Higher % suggests a more affluent school community.
LBOTE
Language Background Other Than English. The percentage of students who speak a language other than English at home. Reflects the cultural diversity of the school.
Grade
A+ ≥1130 · A ≥1100 · A- ≥1070 · B+ ≥1040 · B ≥1010 · B- ≥980 · C+ ≥950 · C ≥920 · C- below. Based on ICSEA score.
Class size
Student to teacher ratio (FTE). Calculated from full-time equivalent enrolments divided by full-time equivalent teaching staff. Lower = smaller classes. National average ~12.9. Source: ACARA 2025.
High demand
Schools with enrolments significantly above average for their type — may have enrolment waitlists. Primary: 700+ students. Secondary: 1,500+ students.
Source: ACARA School Profiles 2025 · Data reflects school community background, not direct academic outcomes.

Why buyers consider Point Cook
Buyers are drawn to Point Cook's top-performing schools, with an ICSEA score of 1088, making it an attractive option for families. The suburb's relatively affordable median house price of $800,000, combined with the $10,000 First Home Owner Grant, makes it an appealing choice for first-home buyers. Point Cook's diverse community, with a strong Indian influence, adds to its appeal, offering a unique cultural experience.
Trade-offs to consider
While Point Cook offers a desirable lifestyle, buyers should be aware of its relatively poor public transport options, with a transit score of 27/100. The suburb's growth corridor status, while beneficial for long-term investment, may also lead to increased prices and competition for buyers. Additionally, the 20.8km distance from Melbourne's CBD may be a drawback for some, requiring a longer commute.

About Point Cook
Point Cook is one of the more populous suburbs in VIC, home to 66,781 people in Melbourne’s middle ring, sitting 20.8km from the CBD. Median household income sits at $2,392 per week — notably higher than the VIC median, reflecting an affluent professional community. The median age of 33 years suggests a mix of young families and established professionals setting down roots.
Schools in Point Cook
School quality in Point Cook is graded A+, based on an average ICSEA score of 1,141 — placing the suburb around the 98th percentile nationally. ICSEA (Index of Community Socio-Educational Advantage) measures the socio-educational background of school communities. The national average is 1,000; the VIC metropolitan median is approximately 1027. Alamanda K-9 College is the highest-rated school within the suburb boundary, a combined school with an ICSEA score of 1,141. There are 11 schools within or immediately adjacent to Point Cook, providing choice across year levels and schooling types. Source: ACARA School Profiles 2025.
Getting around Point Cook
Point Cook has reasonable public transport access, particularly for peak-hour commutes. The suburb scores 51/100 on the GoodSuburb transit index, which is calculated from the density of train, tram and bus stops within the suburb boundary using real GTFS data from PTV (June 2026). At 20.8km from Melbourne CBD, public transport is a viable option for city-bound commuters, though peak hour services vary. The suburb is served by FTTB NBN, providing residential internet connectivity.
Property prices in Point Cook
The median house price in Point Cook is $800k — near the VIC median. Prices have risen 3.9% over the past year. These figures are sourced from the Valuer General Victoria and represent median residential sale prices across all recorded transactions. First home buyers purchasing a new home in Point Cook may be eligible for the First Home Owner Grant of $10,000. Check eligibility and apply at sro.vic.gov.au. Note that the FHOG applies to new and substantially renovated homes only — not established properties.
Who lives in Point Cook
The community in Point Cook is predominantly Australian-born (43.9%), with a notable overseas-born population (56%) reflecting Melbourne’s multicultural character. India-born residents make up 14.9% of the population. The most common industry of employment for Point Cook residents is Professional & Scientific (14.3% of employed residents), followed by Health & Social Services. Owner-occupancy sits at 65.5% — a high rate suggesting a stable, long-term resident community. All community data is sourced from ABS Census 2021.
Is Point Cook right for first home buyers?
For first home buyers, Point Cook is a realistic entry point for buyers working within a tighter budget, scoring 58/100. What these numbers mean
ICSEA
Index of Community Socio-Educational Advantage. Measures the social and educational background of a school's families. National average is 1,000. Higher = more advantaged community.
%ile
Percentile ranking among all Australian schools. 94th %ile means the school's community is more advantaged than 94% of all schools nationally.
Top SEA
Top Socio-Educational Advantage quarter. The percentage of students from the most advantaged 25% of Australian families. Higher % suggests a more affluent school community.
LBOTE
Language Background Other Than English. The percentage of students who speak a language other than English at home. Reflects the cultural diversity of the school.
Grade
A+ ≥1130 · A ≥1100 · A- ≥1070 · B+ ≥1040 · B ≥1010 · B- ≥980 · C+ ≥950 · C ≥920 · C- below. Based on ICSEA score.
Class size
Student to teacher ratio (FTE). Calculated from full-time equivalent enrolments divided by full-time equivalent teaching staff. Lower = smaller classes. National average ~12.9. Source: ACARA 2025.
High demand
Schools with enrolments significantly above average for their type — may have enrolment waitlists. Primary: 700+ students. Secondary: 1,500+ students.
Source: ACARA School Profiles 2025 · Data reflects school community background, not direct academic outcomes.

The median age in Point Cook is 33 years according to the ABS Census 2021. The suburb has a population of 66,781 residents. Median household income is $2,392/week. These figures reflect the 2021 census — the next Australian census is scheduled for 2026.
What schools are in Point Cook?
+
Point Cook has a school grade of A+ based on an average ICSEA of 1,141 (Victorian median: 1,027). Schools include Alamanda K-9 College, Stella Maris Catholic Primary School, Lumen Christi School. ICSEA measures the socio-educational advantage of a school's community — higher scores indicate more advantaged communities. Source: ACARA School Profiles 2025.
Can I get the First Home Owner Grant in Point Cook?
+
Yes. New home purchases in Point Cook may be eligible for the Victorian First Home Owner Grant of $10,000. The grant applies to new or substantially renovated homes only — not established properties. Stamp duty concessions apply for eligible buyers under $750,000. Always verify current eligibility at sro.vic.gov.au before purchasing.
